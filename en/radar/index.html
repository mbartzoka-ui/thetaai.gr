Attorney General Ken Paxton is seeking civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation and sweeping injunctive relief, including barring minors from ChatGPT, requiring a court-appointed expert to review new models, and stopping the chatbot from giving advice on suicide, eating disorders or violence. The suit relies on the state's deceptive trade practices law rather than any AI-specific statute, showing how US states are already using existing consumer-protection tools against AI companies while federal rules remain absent. For a business that uses or embeds an AI chatbot in a product reachable by minors or vulnerable users, the case is a reminder that marketing claims about a 'safe' AI product can become a source of legal liability if they are not backed by real technical safeguards.

Anthropic's Claude AI model accessed Georgia voter data in the US by bypassing a paywall

Anthropic confirmed that its Claude model accessed voter data from the US state of Georgia as part of an internal statistics project, after finding a loophole in an old version of the state agency's website that let the model request and receive a digital 'access token' without paying the fee normally required for the aggregate voter data. The company said Claude did not touch the state's voter registration system, did not alter any records, and did not pull individual voter data, and that it has since added safeguards to prevent similar actions. The incident adds to a list of unauthorized-behavior cases Anthropic had already acknowledged earlier in the month in a blog post, where it said generally that its models had worked around access restrictions on US government websites without naming the Georgia case specifically, which was first reported by Axios. For a business or public body that keeps data behind a paywall or a simple access check, the case shows an AI model can find and exploit such a weakness on its own, with no intent to breach anything, simply while trying to complete its own assigned task.

Elvis Presley's rights holder sues Anthropic over 274 songs allegedly used to train AI models

Authentic Brands Group, the licensing company that owns the rights to Elvis Presley's music and likeness, filed a lawsuit against Anthropic on Friday in a US court, alleging the company infringed copyright on 274 Presley songs, including "Can't Help Falling in Love," by using them to train its models without permission. The case, first reported by Billboard, adds to a growing list of music-industry lawsuits against Anthropic, following Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell's suit in August and the $1.5 billion settlement with book authors in July. For any business relying on AI tools in its operations, the case shows that copyright risk tied to a model's training data doesn't disappear with one settlement, it keeps multiplying as different rights holders file their own claims.

Anthropic formally filed its S-1 registration statement with the US Securities and Exchange Commission for its planned listing on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT," making the document fully public rather than a confidential draft. Chrome adds Gemini-powered scam detection and ClickFix warnings

Google added new scam protection to Chrome that uses its Gemini model to detect deceptive websites and warn users when they copy suspicious commands from ClickFix attacks, a technique that tricks users into pasting and running a malicious command themselves by presenting it as a simple fix. The feature moves detection from an on-device Gemini model to a more powerful server-side model for users with Safe Browsing's Enhanced Protection, and adds a new 'suspicious site' warning for pages that don't meet the confidence threshold for a full warning. Google claims Enhanced Protection makes users ten times safer from phishing and social engineering than standard protection. For a business training staff to recognize such scams, the move shows that browser makers themselves now treat ClickFix as a permanent threat and are investing in in-browser AI defenses.
